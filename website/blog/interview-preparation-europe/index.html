Interview preparation in Europe: choose the right practice task

Start with the interview format and the actual vacancy. This English-language collection covers UK public-sector assessments, graduate exercises and international technical or SaaS team interviews.

Reviewed by Cluegent Editorial Team · Updated October 10, 2026

Choose preparation by format, not just job title

For an interview in Europe, begin with the actual vacancy, your submitted application and the recruiter's instructions. Confirm the language, assessment format and permitted preparation. Then choose an exercise that matches the task you will perform. A generic question list cannot replace practice for a group decision, a code review or a customer demo.

This collection contains this overview and 24 original guides. It is written in English for UK and Ireland applicants and English-speaking international teams in Germany, the Netherlands, Sweden and Denmark. It does not cover every European language, employer or national recruitment system. We have not translated the pages or created copies that differ only by country name.

Every worked scenario is fictional unless explicitly attributed to a source. Suggested timings, checklists and review prompts are our practice aids, not official employer scoring criteria. Use genuine experience in an application and follow the real assessment rules.

Public-sector and graduate interview formats

For UK Civil Service applications, keep strengths reflection separate from behaviours evidence. The official guidance advises against rehearsing strengths answers; behaviours preparation should use the relevant advertised grade and descriptions. For NHS non-clinical roles, use the organisation's actual values rather than an assumed universal script.

Graduate assessment preparation benefits from practising the requested task. Our group exercise asks you to compare options within a fictional budget. The in-tray exercise asks you to prioritise an inbox and assign follow-up actions. The presentation guide helps you structure and defend a recommendation. These tasks serve different preparation needs.

  • Civil Service behaviours interview: build an evidence map
  • NHS values interview questions for non-clinical roles
  • Assessment centre group exercise: a worked practice case
  • In-tray exercise example with a worked prioritisation plan
  • Graduate interview presentation: a practical slide plan

English-language applications and international teams

If you are applying in Germany or Denmark in English, confirm the working language rather than infer it from the posting. Prepare evidence of the actual responsibilities and questions about the team. Avoid national stereotypes: the employer, role and assessment brief are more useful than assumptions about a country's interview personality.

For applicants who work across languages, practise asking concise clarifications. Confirm whether a technical question concerns implementation, operations or a high-level decision before answering. You can communicate accurately without pretending to know a word or claim you cannot explain.

Software, frontend, data and platform exercises

Use a concrete artefact to test your reasoning: a README, review comment, incident timeline, form, migration plan or architecture decision record. Explain what you know, what remains uncertain and which test would change your conclusion. These guides are relevant to international engineering roles; they are not evidence that a particular country's employers all use the same questions.

  • Code review interview practice: prioritise a booking patch
  • Pair programming interview practice: handle a changing requirement
  • Debugging interview practice: investigate a duplicate order
  • Frontend accessibility interview: review a booking form
  • Database migration interview: change a field without breaking clients
  • Incident postmortem interview: turn a timeline into an action plan
  • Release manager interview: explain a go/no-go decision
  • Data analyst case study interview: explain conflicting dashboards
  • Product manager prioritisation interview: a worked decision case

Customer-facing technical and SaaS interviews

Customer-facing cases require both diagnosis and communication. Practise distinguishing what the customer reports from what the evidence proves. Agree the outcome and responsibilities before promising a feature, launch date or commercial concession. Prepare separate technical and customer summaries with consistent facts.

  • API integration interview: investigate a customer's duplicate records
  • Customer success interview case study: a renewal-risk account
  • Solutions engineer demo interview: show a relevant customer outcome
  • Implementation consultant interview: plan a client kickoff

Turn a guide into a preparation session

  2. Read the exercise and answer before viewing the worked reasoning.
  3. Ask a partner for a follow-up that challenges an assumption.
  4. Compare your response using the free mock interview scorecard.
  5. Replace fictional examples with truthful experience where the question requires it.
