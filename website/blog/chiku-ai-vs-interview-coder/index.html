Chiku AI vs Interview Coder: Compare the Workflow Before You Pay

The useful question is not which homepage makes the stronger promise. It is which workflow fits your interview format, practice schedule, and ability to explain an answer yourself.

Reviewed by Cluegent Editorial Team · Updated September 19, 2026

Disclosure: Cluegent offers a competing product. This guide checks public vendor documentation, not hands-on performance. Product details were checked September 19, 2026; confirm current entitlements before paying. The exercises below are our own practice suggestions.

What do the official sites actually offer?

Chiku AI presents interview assistance across coding and general interview scenarios, advertises per-minute billing, and lists multilingual transcription. Interview Coder markets a technical-interview assistant with audio support and coverage that includes coding and system design. These are vendor descriptions, not results from a controlled comparison.

Both sites make strong claims about their products. We have not established that one is faster, more accurate, or universally hidden during screen sharing. Platform versions, permissions, the meeting application, and the assessment rules all matter. Treat those issues as questions to test with permission, not as guarantees supplied by a comparison article.

Choose around the work you need to do

If your next round mixes project discussion with short technical questions, evaluate how a tool handles context switches. If the round is a long coding problem, prioritize whether you can follow an explanation, state complexity, and test edge cases. If it is architecture-heavy, judge whether the response asks for requirements before drawing components.

Make a small requirements sheet before visiting a checkout page. Include your operating system, microphone setup, preferred language, expected session length, and whether you need practice, live assistance, or both. Mark each requirement as essential or optional. An appealing extra feature should not compensate for failure on the one workflow you actually need.

Use the same coding exercise for both evaluations

Our suggested exercise is a small request deduplicator: given request IDs in arrival order, return only their first occurrences while preserving order. Begin with an empty input, repeated IDs, and IDs that differ only in case. Decide explicitly whether IDs are case-sensitive. Then explain the difference between storing seen IDs and repeatedly scanning the output.

A strong answer defines the contract, chooses a set for membership checks, preserves output order, and explains expected linear time with linear additional storage. Follow up by asking what changes for an unbounded stream. A memory limit or retention window changes the requirements; silently dropping old IDs changes what deduplication guarantees. Score this reasoning rather than the length of generated code.

Compare costs using your real practice calendar

For any usage-metered service, estimate the minutes spent rehearsing plus time spent setting up and reviewing inside a session. For a subscription, estimate completed useful sessions before renewal. Verify whether silence, retries, or an interrupted session consume allowance. Do not treat a download button as proof of free AI usage.

Here is a simple budgeting example, not a statement of either vendor's price: four thirty-minute sessions require two hours of practice allowance. Add two short setup checks if they are billable. Write down the actual checkout total for that workload, the cancellation deadline, and the refund conditions. Compare the same workload on both products.

Make a decision with a pass/fail threshold

Require correct handling of your three test cases, an explanation you can repeat unaided, an acceptable setup experience, and clear billing. Keep notes on failures as well as successes. One impressive response is not enough evidence to generalize across all your interviews, and an AI response should still be checked for correctness.

Only use assistance in an assessed interview when the interview rules allow it.
