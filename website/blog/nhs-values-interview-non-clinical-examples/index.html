Use the recruiting organisation's actual values

For an NHS values interview, connect the recruiting organisation's published values to specific actions from your experience. NHS Employers describes values-based recruitment as aligning personal values and behaviours with NHS values. Check the job pack and organisation website for the wording relevant to your application.

This guide is for administration, reception and other non-clinical applicants. The practice scenario concerns communication and escalation, not medical care. Recruitment arrangements and published values differ between organisations and UK nations; a generic list is not a substitute for the actual vacancy.

Practise a reception communication scenario

In our fictional exercise, someone arrives for an appointment and cannot understand the directions they were given. The waiting area is busy, and another visitor interrupts. Explain how you would listen, clarify the administrative issue and obtain appropriate help without making clinical decisions.

First ask what the person needs and check what information you are authorised to access. Avoid discussing personal details across the waiting room. If the issue goes beyond your role, explain how you would contact the appropriate colleague and keep the person informed.

Show care through practical actions

An illustrative response might begin: 'I would let the person explain the difficulty, then check the appointment instructions through the approved process. I would use clear language and ask whether my explanation made sense. If I could not resolve it, I would contact the appropriate colleague rather than guess.' The emphasis is on actions within the role.

For an experience question, use something you actually did in a shop, office, voluntary setting or previous administrative role. Explain the person's need and your contribution. Do not invent patient contact or imply clinical competence because the vacancy is in healthcare.

Follow-up questions to test your reasoning

  • What if the visitor remains upset?
  • What information should not be discussed publicly?
  • When would you ask a colleague for help?

Check whether your answer contains a visible act of listening, an appropriate boundary and a next step. Saying 'I am compassionate' leaves the evidence unstated. The organisation's policies and role responsibilities should determine the action in a real situation.

Build a small bank of non-clinical evidence

Choose examples of respectful service, teamwork and acknowledging a mistake. Label each with the relevant published value and explain what you learnt. These are our preparation prompts, not a list of questions an NHS panel is guaranteed to ask.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use examples from outside the NHS?

Use truthful experience relevant to the role and question. Explain transferable actions without presenting non-clinical experience as clinical training.

Do all NHS organisations use identical interview questions?

Do not assume that. Follow the job pack, the organisation's values and the recruiter's format instructions.
