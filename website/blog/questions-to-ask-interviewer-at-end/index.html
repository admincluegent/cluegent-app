Questions to Ask in an Interview

The questions you ask should help you evaluate the role and demonstrate that you understand its real work, expectations, and tradeoffs.

Reviewed by Cluegent Editorial Team · Updated August 18, 2026

Choose questions for the person in front of you

A recruiter, future manager, engineer, and executive can answer different questions well. Prepare more questions than you need, then choose two or three based on what the interview has already covered.

Questions about success and scope

  • What would a strong first 90 days look like?
  • What problem needs the most attention from the person joining this role?
  • How will success be measured during the first year?
  • Which responsibilities are fixed, and which may change?

Questions for a hiring manager

  • How do you give feedback and support professional growth?
  • What distinguishes people who perform well on this team?
  • Where does the team need more ownership or expertise?
  • How are priorities handled when several projects are urgent?

Questions about team culture and collaboration

  • How does the team share context when priorities or requirements change?
  • What does healthy disagreement look like on this team?
  • How do people collaborate across functions or time zones?
  • What has the team changed recently based on employee feedback?
  • How are important decisions documented and communicated?

Questions about learning and career growth

  • What skills would I be expected to develop during the first year?
  • How do people receive coaching or feedback outside formal reviews?
  • Can you share an example of how someone has grown in this team?
  • How are stretch assignments or new responsibilities decided?
  • What learning resources or mentorship are available?

Questions for an engineer or technical interviewer

  • What technical decision has the team reconsidered recently?
  • How do code review, testing, deployment, and incident response work?
  • Where does technical debt create the most friction?
  • How much time is spent building new features versus maintaining systems?

Questions to avoid or reframe

Avoid asking something answered clearly on the first page of the company website. Do not ask five questions at once, and do not turn the final minutes into a second sales pitch. Compensation, leave, and flexibility are legitimate topics, but direct them to the person responsible for those details and choose the right stage.

Questions about next steps

  • Is there anything in my background that you would like me to clarify?
  • What are the remaining stages in the interview process?
  • What timeline are you working toward for the decision?

Ask about next steps after your role-specific questions. It gives the interviewer a chance to surface concerns and gives you a clear expectation for follow-up.

Build one question from the conversation

The strongest final question often begins with something you heard: “You mentioned the team is migrating the reporting pipeline. What has been the hardest part of that transition?” Cluegent's rolling context can help you note possible follow-ups during permitted calls, but the final question should reflect genuine curiosity.

Frequently asked questions

How many questions should I ask at the end of an interview?

Usually two or three thoughtful questions fit the available time. Prepare at least five so you have alternatives when topics are already covered.

Is it acceptable to ask about salary?

Yes, but ask the recruiter or hiring manager at an appropriate stage. A technical interviewer may not know the compensation details.

What if the interviewer answered all my prepared questions?

Ask a follow-up based on the conversation, or ask what they personally find most challenging or rewarding about the team.
