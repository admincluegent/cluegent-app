Customer success interview case study: a renewal-risk account

Understand the customer's goal before proposing a discount

In a customer success case interview, separate declining product activity from confirmed renewal risk. Ask what the customer was trying to achieve, what changed and who makes the renewal decision. A usage chart alone cannot tell you whether the product delivered the intended value.

This original SaaS case is for applicants to international customer-facing teams in Europe, including Ireland and the Netherlands. It concerns account discovery and a renewal conversation, not a claim about salaries, market size or how every employer runs interviews.

Investigate a fictional account with fewer active users

The fictional customer has fewer active users than last month and a renewal meeting approaching. Two unresolved support issues appear in the account notes. The original sponsor has moved teams, and no current success criteria are recorded. You do not have authority to promise a discount or a product feature.

Prepare the first conversation and an internal plan. Identify which facts you would verify before presenting a recovery proposal. Do not convert the usage decline into a guaranteed cancellation or claim a product defect caused it without evidence.

Renewal discovery worksheet
SignalQuestion and next action
Usage declineWhich users and workflows changed, and why?
Open support issuesWhat impact remains and who owns resolution?
Sponsor changeWho now owns the business goal and decision?
Missing success criteriaWhat outcome would make renewal worthwhile to the customer?

Propose a meeting that produces an agreed plan

An illustrative opening is: 'I would first confirm the customer's current goal and whether the lower activity reflects a problem or a changed workflow. I would review the support issues with their owners before the meeting. Then I would agree the most important next actions with the customer and confirm who evaluates renewal.' This establishes discovery before a commercial offer.

If a support fix is uncertain, provide an accurate status and an owner instead of promising a date. Suggest a review point with evidence the customer considers meaningful. A plan might focus on one important workflow rather than an unrequested training programme for every user.

Follow-up questions to test your reasoning

  • What if reduced activity reflects successful automation?
  • Who needs to join the next meeting?
  • What can you commit to without product or commercial approval?

Check whether your recommendation would change after discovering a different customer goal. If it remains 'offer training and a discount' in every case, the plan is not responding to evidence. Make the action, owner and expected customer outcome explicit.

Role-play an uncertain sponsor conversation

Ask a partner to play a new sponsor who does not know the original buying decision. Practise asking for context without making the customer repeat every historical detail. Follow up with a brief written plan that distinguishes confirmed commitments from proposed actions.

Frequently asked questions

What makes a useful customer success case answer?

Clarify the customer's goal, verify signals, identify stakeholders and propose actions with owners and agreed evidence.

Should I offer a discount to a renewal-risk customer?

Do not assume price is the cause or promise terms without authority. Understand the concern and coordinate with the appropriate commercial owner.
