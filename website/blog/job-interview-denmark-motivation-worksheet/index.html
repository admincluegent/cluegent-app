Prepare for the vacancy rather than national stereotypes

For a job interview in Denmark, explain why the specific work interests you and how your experience supports it. Confirm the interview language and format with the recruiter. This worksheet is for English-speaking applicants; it does not claim that all Danish employers ask the same questions or prefer one personality type.

A reason such as 'Denmark seems like a good place to live' may be true but leaves the role unexplained. Keep personal location preferences separate from your evidence of fit. Use the employer's posting to identify what you would actually work on.

Turn the job description into a motivation map

Imagine a fictional implementation role at a business software company in Copenhagen. The posting mentions client onboarding, data imports and coordination with support. You have experience checking imported files and explaining issues to colleagues, but have not led a full client implementation.

Write one sentence about the work you want to do, one about a relevant action you have already taken, and one about the skill you still need to develop. Do not turn an interest in onboarding into a false claim that you have managed enterprise deployments.

Role-motivation worksheet
Vacancy detailPersonal evidence or question
Client onboardingA real example of helping someone understand a process.
Data importsA file check or discrepancy you personally investigated.
Coordination with supportHow you handed over an issue and confirmed ownership.
Working arrangementsAsk about location, team communication and the working language.

Give a motivation answer with a learning boundary

An illustrative answer is: 'I am interested in the combination of data work and explaining a process to clients. In my previous role I checked incoming files and worked with support when records did not match. I have not led a full implementation, so I would want to understand how the team supports someone taking on that responsibility.' The boundary is part of the answer.

You can then ask about the first project, the handover between sales and implementation, and who reviews data-mapping decisions. These questions help you understand the actual role. They are not claims about how every employer in Denmark operates.

Follow-up questions to test your reasoning

  • Which responsibility interests you most, and why?
  • What experience transfers to that responsibility?
  • What would you need to learn during the first project?

Check whether the answer could be sent unchanged to any company. If so, add a genuine connection to this posting. Remove unsupported praise such as 'market-leading culture' unless you can explain its source and relevance to the work.

Prepare questions that help both sides assess fit

Ask which language is used for client communication, how responsibilities are divided and what the next interview evaluates. Discuss relocation and practical arrangements with the recruiter using accurate personal details. This exercise provides no immigration or employment-law advice.

Frequently asked questions

What should I prepare for an interview in Denmark?

Start with the vacancy, your submitted application, relevant evidence and the recruiter's format instructions. Confirm the working language and practical arrangements.

Is there a standard Danish motivation answer?

No answer fits every employer. Explain your genuine interest in the actual responsibilities and acknowledge skills you still need to develop.
