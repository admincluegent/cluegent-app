Implementation consultant interview: plan a client kickoff

For an implementation consultant interview, clarify scope, responsibilities and acceptance before committing to a delivery plan. A requested launch date is a constraint to investigate, not proof that the work can be completed by then.

Reviewed by Cluegent Editorial Team · Updated October 10, 2026

Agree the outcome before committing to a launch

For an implementation consultant interview, clarify scope, responsibilities and acceptance before committing to a delivery plan. A requested launch date is a constraint to investigate, not proof that the work can be completed by then.

This original client kickoff case is for applicants to international software teams across Europe. It concerns delivery planning and customer communication. It does not claim that every implementation uses the same stages or that Cluegent manages customer projects.

Plan an import with unresolved ownership

A fictional client wants a new system ready for its next reporting cycle. The data export contains inconsistent account identifiers. Sales has discussed a launch date, but the client has not named a data owner and the acceptance criteria are missing. You can coordinate the project but cannot change the contract.

Prepare a kickoff agenda that exposes the dependency on clean identifiers. Identify what can proceed while the client confirms ownership. Ask who approves the imported result and what they will compare it with. Do not promise that a data cleanup tool can infer all correct matches.

Kickoff agenda worksheet
Agenda itemAgreement to obtain
OutcomeWhich reporting workflow must work at launch?
ScopeWhich data and features are included or excluded?
OwnershipWho resolves identifier problems and signs off the result?
ReadinessWhat sample data proves the import can be tested?
AcceptanceWhich checks determine whether the delivered workflow is ready?

Give a plan with a visible dependency

A worked answer is: 'I would confirm the reporting outcome and the agreed scope first. We can prepare mapping and a test import, but identifier resolution needs a client owner. I would make that dependency visible in the plan and agree acceptance checks before treating the proposed launch date as confirmed.' The plan distinguishes preparation from a commitment.

If the client insists on the original date, explain the options and consequences within your authority. A smaller agreed scope may be possible, or the date may need review. Escalate commercial changes to the responsible person rather than silently changing scope.

Follow-up questions to test your reasoning

  • What work can continue before the data owner is named?
  • Who approves a change in scope?
  • What happens if the test import reveals unmatched accounts?

Check that every important dependency has an owner and a completion condition. 'Waiting for client data' is less useful than identifying the exact sample, validation and decision required. Make risks understandable to both technical and business participants.

Write a kickoff summary the client can act on

After the role-play, write agreed scope, open questions, owners and next checks. Ask a partner to identify their action from the note. Use the Denmark motivation worksheet if you also need to explain why an implementation role matches your background.

Frequently asked questions

What should I discuss in an implementation kickoff case?

Clarify the desired outcome, scope, owners, readiness, dependencies and acceptance criteria before committing to delivery.

Should I promise the requested launch date?

Only within your authority and after the relevant scope and dependencies are understood. State uncertainty and escalate decisions appropriately.
