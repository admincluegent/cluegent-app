Your recruiter phone screen checklist

Before a recruiter phone screen, confirm the job and call details, prepare a short introduction backed by your resume, and write down your compensation and availability questions. Rehearse aloud. Keep a compact reference sheet so you can answer directly without reading a script.

This guide is for people applying to US roles. The examples use US dollars, American resume terminology and explicit time-zone labels. Confirm the actual interview format with your recruiter.

  1. Save the job description and the resume version you submitted.
  2. Confirm the date, time zone, meeting link and who initiates the call.
  3. Choose two examples that demonstrate the role's important requirements.
  4. Practice your introduction and explain why this particular role interests you.
  5. Prepare compensation, work-arrangement and start-date questions.
  6. Test your microphone and keep a backup contact route.
  7. Clarify the next round and its preparation requirements.

A note that says “great communicator” gives you little to use. Replace it with the audience you worked with, the decision you explained and what happened. Leave a metric out when you cannot verify it. A clear description is better than a fabricated percentage.

Clarify US compensation and scheduling details

A number such as $100,000 can mean annual base pay or part of a total compensation package. Ask which components are included. This is an illustrative amount, not a market salary recommendation. Use the actual employer range and research the role, level and location.

You can ask: “Could you share the budgeted base salary range and how bonus or equity fits into the package?” Specify USD and annual base salary when describing your expectations. Our salary expectations guide provides practice structures.

Write availability as dates and labeled time zones. “Tuesday at 10” leaves room for error. Confirm the calendar invitation rather than assuming ET, PT and your local time are interchangeable. Ask whether “remote” means anywhere or within locations approved by the employer.

Answer work-authorization and sponsorship questions accurately. This worksheet does not determine eligibility or provide immigration advice. Clarify uncertain details with an appropriate qualified adviser and the employer instead of using an AI-generated guess.

Prepare an introduction you can defend

Build your introduction around your current work, one relevant example and why the opening makes sense for you. Aim for about a minute during practice, then adjust to the question. This is a rehearsal target, not a hiring rule.

A fictional backend-applicant example: “I work on API services for a subscription product. My recent project involved coordinating a database migration with support and engineering. This role interests me because it combines backend ownership with customer-facing reliability work.” Replace every detail with your own experience.

Prepare a follow-up for each claim. What did you personally decide? What constraint changed the plan? What would you improve next time? Revise any sentence you cannot explain after a follow-up.

Run a 12-minute recruiter-screen rehearsal

Our suggested exercise allocates two minutes to your introduction and motivation, four to evidence examples, three to practical alignment and three to reviewing vague answers. The total is 12 minutes; employers set their own call length.

Practice with a friend, a tool or typed prompts. Ask one question at a time and answer aloud before viewing suggested wording. Use this prompt:

Act as a recruiter for the job description I provide. Ask about my background, then follow up on one relevant example. Ask why I want the role and which practical details need clarification. Use only facts from my resume. Let me answer before offering feedback, and flag unsupported claims.

Test your setup and clarify the next step

Harvard's career guidance recommends a quiet location, clear delivery and preparation for phone interviews. Test the device you will use. Keep the callback number or backup email ready for a disconnected call.

Ask what the next interview evaluates and whether preparation materials are available. Record who will contact you and when. Our follow-up email examples help you write a brief, specific note.

Rehearsing beforehand is separate from using AI during a real assessment. Follow the employer's rules and required consent for transcription or recording. If assistance is prohibited, close the assistant and use your own preparation.

Frequently asked questions

How do I prepare for a recruiter phone screen?

Confirm the call details, save your submitted resume and job description, choose evidence of role fit, rehearse a short introduction, and prepare compensation, availability and next-step questions.

Is this recruiter phone screen worksheet free?

Yes. Copy or print it without signing up. Cluegent's desktop app is a separate product with a limited trial and paid plans.

Is a recruiter screen the same as a technical interview?

Not necessarily. A recruiter may focus on fit and logistics, while another round assesses technical depth. Ask what this particular call will cover.

Can I use AI during the recruiter call?

Only where the employer permits it and required consent is obtained. Practice beforehand; complete an assessment that prohibits outside assistance without the assistant.
