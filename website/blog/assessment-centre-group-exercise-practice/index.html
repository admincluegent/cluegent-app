Practise reaching a decision with other candidates

An assessment centre group exercise gives you a shared problem to discuss and resolve. Practise explaining a recommendation, listening to other proposals and helping the group make progress. gradireland's assessment-centre guidance describes group exercises alongside other tasks; your invitation determines the actual format.

Our original case works with friends or a study group. It has no single official answer. The aim is to make your reasoning and collaboration visible rather than compete to speak most often. Ask the recruiter about permitted preparation materials before a real assessment.

Allocate a fictional campus support budget

Your group has an illustrative EUR 12,000 budget for a student support pilot. Consider three options: extended evening advice sessions costing EUR 8,000, an appointment reminder service costing EUR 4,000, and a quiet study refurbishment costing EUR 10,000. These figures are invented for the exercise, not real supplier prices.

The brief says evening enquiries are unanswered, missed appointments waste capacity, and students have asked for quieter space. You have no verified demand counts. Recommend a package within budget, identify the missing evidence and agree how to evaluate the pilot. Set a practice limit of fifteen minutes if your group wants a time constraint.

Practice options and uncertainties
OptionQuestion before choosing
Evening advice: EUR 8,000Who needs it, and when would staff be available?
Reminders: EUR 4,000Why are appointments being missed?
Quiet study: EUR 10,000Is noise the main barrier, and who would benefit?
Budget: EUR 12,000Which combination fits and what does it leave unfunded?

Give a provisional recommendation with a condition

One possible recommendation funds advice sessions plus reminders: EUR 8,000 + EUR 4,000 = EUR 12,000. Explain that this addresses two problems in the brief, then acknowledge that demand remains uncertain. A different choice can be defensible if the group explains who benefits and how it will test the assumption.

Try language such as: 'Could we agree the criteria before choosing? I suggest expected benefit, evidence and feasibility. We have a budget-fitting combination, but we need to check whether missed appointments are caused by forgotten dates or another barrier.' This contributes a method without taking over.

Follow-up questions to test your reasoning

  • Which proposal would change if the budget fell?
  • How would you include a quieter group member?
  • What evidence would overturn the recommendation?

Afterwards, each participant identifies one contribution that moved the decision forward and one occasion when they interrupted or ignored useful evidence. Record the group decision and unresolved assumptions. A unanimous decision is less useful practice if nobody examined its weaknesses.

Repeat with a different role in the discussion

On the next attempt, try summarising instead of opening the discussion. Invite an alternative view and check the arithmetic aloud. Use the mock scorecard for feedback on clarity and listening; its ratings are for practice and do not reproduce any employer's marks.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need to lead the assessment centre group?

Practise making useful contributions, listening and supporting a clear decision. Do not assume that speaking most or naming yourself leader is the assessed goal.

Is there one correct answer to this case?

No. This original exercise allows different defensible choices. Explain the constraints, assumptions and evaluation plan.
