Scrum Master Interview Questions: Coaching and Delivery Scenarios

Prepare Scrum Master interview answers on the Sprint Goal, impediments, retrospectives and stakeholder pressure using an original mid-sprint change scenario.

Reviewed by Cluegent Editorial Team · Updated October 3, 2026

Prepare Scrum Master interview answers on the Sprint Goal, impediments, retrospectives and stakeholder pressure using an original mid-sprint change scenario. Start with the question, explain the mechanism, and then state an assumption or tradeoff. The scenarios below are original practice examples, not questions supplied by an employer.

What is the Scrum Master's responsibility?

The Scrum Master is accountable for establishing Scrum as defined in the Scrum Guide and helping people understand and use it. Explain coaching, facilitation and improvement of effectiveness rather than positioning the role as a task dispatcher. Give a real example of making a barrier visible and helping the right people resolve it. Avoid claiming that the Scrum Master alone controls all delivery decisions.

How would you handle an impediment?

Clarify its effect, who can resolve it and what the team has already tried. Help make the problem visible and remove unnecessary organizational barriers. An unclear acceptance decision may require a conversation rather than escalation to a senior manager. Explain how you know the impediment is resolved: the blocked work can proceed and the underlying decision or dependency is understood.

What if a stakeholder requests urgent work during a Sprint?

Ask what changed, how urgent the need is and how it affects the Sprint Goal. Facilitate a conversation with the Product Owner and Developers about scope and consequences. Do not promise extra work without discussing capacity, and do not reject every change as inherently forbidden. Explain the tradeoff and preserve transparency about what can still be achieved.

How do you make a retrospective useful?

Choose a specific observed problem, create space for different perspectives and agree a small improvement with a way to evaluate it. A long list of complaints without action rarely changes work. For example, a team might test earlier review of acceptance criteria for two weeks. At the next retrospective, compare the resulting misunderstandings and rework instead of merely asking whether people enjoyed the meeting.

How should you discuss velocity in an interview?

Treat a team's historical estimates as planning information with context. Do not compare story points across teams as if they were standardized units of productivity. Ask what the metric is being used to decide and what behavior it might encourage. Explain customer outcomes, quality and predictability alongside planning information. A team increasing points while accumulating defects may not be improving its effectiveness.

Worked example

Original scenario: halfway through a Sprint, a customer reports an important defect. The team has a Sprint Goal around improving onboarding. First clarify the defect's impact and whether it changes that goal or simply changes which work is needed to reach it. Bring the Product Owner and Developers into the decision.

Describe possible scope adjustments and make the consequence visible to stakeholders. Avoid answering with 'the team must work overtime.' Afterward, explore whether the defect escaped through unclear criteria, missing checks or another cause, and propose a small improvement based on that evidence.

Practice plan

Prepare a two-minute answer to the defect scenario and a separate answer about an ineffective retrospective. Name your contribution without claiming ownership of every team result. Read the Scrum Guide's accountability definitions and explain how the people in your example made the decision together.

Frequently asked questions

Is the Scrum Master the team's project manager?

The Scrum Guide defines a distinct accountability. Explain facilitation and coaching while making the Product Owner's and Developers' responsibilities clear.

Should I compare teams using velocity?

Story-point scales differ between teams. Explain the decision you need to make and use evidence that is meaningful in that context.
